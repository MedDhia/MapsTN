# Depicted features and regional coverage

Coding of all 663 records. Generated by `scripts/code_features_regions.py`; variables are defined in [CODEBOOK-FEATURES.md](CODEBOOK-FEATURES.md).

## Why this could not be done from metadata

Wells, shrines and mosques are drawn on maps; they are not catalogued. Across all 663 records the Dublin Core names **mosques in 0 records, tribes in 0, oases in 0, cemeteries in 0 and wells in 5**. Any coding built only on catalogue text would report that this collection shows none of these things, which is false.

So the feature coding is a **scale-and-series model calibrated against sheets inspected directly through IIIF**. Every row records its basis in `features_basis`: `image` for the sheets actually examined, `scale_model` for the inference.

## What the sheets actually show

**Nouvelle carte de la Tunisie (Taride)** (1920, 1:900 000) — [view](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b530647344)

Carries a full 'Signes conventionnels' panel plus an 'Explication des principaux termes arabes' glossary: Aïn/Aïoun/Bir = puits, source; Bordj = poste fortifié; Kalâa/Ksar = fort, village fortifié; Koubba = chapelle; Zaouia = chapelle; Sidi = saint; Souk = marché; Oued = rivière; Chott/Sebkra = lac salé. Settlement ranks run Capitale / Ville importante / Ville secondaire / Village ou centre important / Hameau, village indigène. Distinct symbols for mines de fer, zinc et plomb, phosphate, cuivre, and a 'Limite nord du Territoire Militaire'.

**Carte de la Régence de Tunis (Garnier Frères)** (1881, 1:500 000) — [view](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b53021186j)

Carries an 'Explication des termes géographiques' with separate Arabic and Kabyle glossaries, and a descriptive panel 'Tunis et la Tunisie'.

**Carte de la Tunisie, Service géographique de l'armée** (1889, 1:800 000) — [view](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b53024975r)

No conventional-signs panel; relief by hachures.

**Carte de la Tunisie, Service géographique de l'armée** (1895, 1:800 000) — [view](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b53121118t)

Legend covers railways only: lignes en exploitation, en construction, étudiées.

**Environs de Medenine** (1911, 1:50 000) — [view](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b8445503p)

No conventional-signs panel, but the sign vocabulary is legible on the sheet: wells as dense blue circles, marabouts as red dome symbols beside 'Sdi ...' names, 'Kba Sdi ben Bekr' for a koubba, 'Biar ez Ziria' for a well group, ksour and guerar granary clusters, and a separately labelled 'Village Français' beside the indigenous centre of Medenine.

**Environs du Kef** (n.d., 1:50 000) — [view](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b53060313z)

Abbreviations readable on the sheet: Mvet = marabout (red symbol), Za = zaouia, Ae = aïn (spring), 'Puits' written out, Kat = kalâa (fortified height), Hr = henchir (ruined estate), Dj = djebel, Chabe = ravine. Cultivation shown by red hatching, carob and olive by tree symbols.

**Manoeuvres d'Algérie-Tunisie** (1929, 1:200 000) — [view](https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b53066569j)

Full labelled graticule at 30-minute intervals; relief in colour with contours at 20 m.

The 1920 Taride sheet is the most useful of these, because it carries its own glossary of Arabic feature generics — the map documents the very vocabulary this coding looks for: *Aïn / Bir* = well or spring, *Bordj* = fortified post, *Kalâa / Ksar* = fort or fortified village, *Koubba* and *Zaouia* = chapel, *Sidi* = saint, *Souk* = market, *Oued* = river, *Chott / Sebkra* = salt lake. Its settlement hierarchy runs Capitale, Ville importante, Ville secondaire, Village ou centre important, Hameau or village indigène — and it marks a **Limite nord du Territoire Militaire**, the boundary of the militarily administered south.

At 1:50 000 the sign vocabulary is far richer: the Kef sheet uses `Mvet` for marabout, `Za` for zaouia, `Ae` for aïn, `Kat` for kalâa and `Hr` for henchir, with *Puits* written out. The Medenine sheet shows wells as dense blue circles, marabouts as red dome symbols, a koubba (`Kba Sdi ben Bekr`), ksour and guerar granary clusters — and a separately labelled **Village Français** beside the indigenous centre, which is the settler/indigenous distinction drawn directly on the map.
